### What is a Digital Garden?
A digital garden is a space on the internet where thoughts, notes, and reflections are cultivated over time. Unlike blogs that emphasize chronology and polish, gardens emphasize connection, iteration, and depth. A note from last year might still be relevant today — and might even grow into something richer. It's less about publishing and more about **growing**.
In my case, it's a collection of interconnected notes — ideas I’m exploring, things I’ve learned, philosophies I’m questioning, and even half-formed thoughts I might come back to later. It’s a blend of public journaling, learning in the open, and a non-linear web of insights.
### Why I'm Building It
### 1. **To Think More Clearly**
Writing is how I think. It’s how I untangle complexity and make sense of the world. By making my thinking visible, I force myself to slow down and wrestle with my assumptions. This isn’t about sounding smart — it’s about being honest with myself. The garden becomes a mirror.
### 2. **To Embrace Imperfection**
So much of what we put online is driven by pressure to impress. A digital garden, by contrast, invites imperfection. It gives me permission to share “unfinished” thoughts and refine them later. That iterative nature is liberating. It echoes how real learning works — nonlinear, messy, full of edits.
### 3. **To Build in Public**
I believe in the power of learning out loud. Sharing what I’m exploring — whether it’s ethics, technology, or mindfulness — not only helps others who might be on similar paths, but invites conversation. The garden becomes less of a monologue and more of a dialogical ecosystem.
### 4. **To Connect Ideas Across Domains**
As someone who spans multiple domains — programming, statistics, mindfulness, ethics, entrepreneurship — I needed a space where cross-pollination is not just allowed but encouraged. A blog tends to silo ideas. A garden lets them mingle. My note on "free will" might connect to one on "AI alignment." A reflection on gender might weave into a thought on user-centered design. It's all connected — and the garden makes that visible.
### 5. **To Take Ownership of My Knowledge**
We spend so much time learning, reading, listening — but how much of that gets captured, revisited, or connected to our other knowledge? The garden is a personal knowledge base, but unlike private notes in Notion or Obsidian, it’s built to be shared. It’s my attempt to own what I’ve learned and contribute something back to the commons.
---
### What You’ll Find in My Garden
You’ll find notes on:
- Morality, consciousness, and what it means to be good
- Musings on software design and technical patterns I’m exploring
- Reflections on entrepreneurship, leadership, and the hidden curriculum of being a CTO
- Notes on books, podcasts, and papers I’m engaging with
- Half-formed questions, not answers
You won’t find perfectly crafted essays, SEO-optimized posts, or click-driven titles (unless I’m being sarcastic). What you will find is **me**, evolving, thinking, documenting.
---
### Final Thoughts
I don’t know what the garden will become. And that’s the point.
It’s a space for **slow thinking**, for quiet growth, for cultivating depth in an internet that often rewards shallowness. It’s a commitment to curiosity over certainty, and a belief that thinking in public can be both humble and powerful.
So if you stumble into my garden, welcome. The paths are winding. The notes are messy. But maybe — just maybe — you’ll find something that resonates, something that sparks your own reflection.
Let’s grow ideas, not just content.
